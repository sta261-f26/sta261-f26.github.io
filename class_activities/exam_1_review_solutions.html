Short answer

  1. Describe some differences between data frames in R vs. pandas.

Solution: In terms of the objects themselves, the biggest difference is that DataFrames in pandas (like Series) always have an index. This means that there are multiple ways to access entries in a pandas DataFrame, and different data wrangling operations will interact differently with that index.

In terms of how we can use data frames when writing code, there are some other differences which are more reflective of differences between R and Python, as opposed to object specifics. For example, R supports tidy evaluation, which means that df |> group_by(col1) works because R knows that col1 must refer to a column in the data frame. In pandas, those column names would always have to be in quotes. E.g. df.groupby('col1')

Data analysis: water quality at Sydney beaches

High rainfall during summer 2025 caused concern about the water quality of beaches in Sydney, Australia.

To explore the relationship between rainfall and water quality, we have measurements on both water quality and weather data from 1991 to 2025.

Research question: How is rainfall related to E-coli bacteria levels?

Possible solution

When planning a data analysis, I think it helps to work backward from where we want to be. We want to know whether rainfall is related to E. coli bacteria levels.

This means that we need rainfall and E. coli levels as two different columns in the same dataset, with each row a location at a date and time. Our data should look something like this:

swim site date time enterococci_cfu_100ml precipitation_mm latitude longitude water_temperature_c …
… … … … … … … … …

We could then make a scatterplot, with E. coli levels on the y axis and precipitation on the x axis, and use correlation to describe the relationship.

To further investigate, I would suggest fitting a model, with E. coli as the response and precipitation as one of the explanatory variables. Since other factors may also be related to water quality, we would want to account for other explanatory variables (water temperature, water authority, etc.) in our model.

So how do we get there? We should join (I think an inner join would make the most sense, since we need both E. coli and precipitation information) water_quality and weather on date, and possibly on longitude and latitude, depending on how the data is recorded. If we want to join on longitude and latitude, we may need to identify the closest longitude and latitude, since the values may not exactly match between the two tables.
